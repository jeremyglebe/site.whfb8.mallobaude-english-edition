Introduction

The Revolt of Mallobaude is a two-player campaign supplement set during the prelude to the End Times. It can be played independently or alongside the other End Times supplements, allowing you to replay - and perhaps alter - the final days of the Warhammer world.

This volume is not self-contained. Use it alongside the companion Game Guide, which describes the course of the campaign independently of the rules system used to play it.

This volume contains all the rules needed to play the campaign with Warhammer Fantasy Battles 8th Edition.

It introduces new characters, units, and army lists representing the forces that fought during the Revolt of Mallobaude. They can be used both in the campaign and in other games of Warhammer.

Most of these game elements can be reused outside the campaign. The rules are modular, so you may use them separately or combine them as you wish.

This is an unofficial supplement, so agree with your opponents before using it. You are also free to alter any part of it if doing so improves your gaming experience.

How to Use This Supplement

This rules supplement focuses on the Revolt of Mallobaude. It contains:

Civil War Rules: Adapted from the official rules published in White Dwarf, these allow battles between armies chosen from the same army book - in this campaign, Bretonnia and Vampire Counts.

New Units and Characters: Rules are provided for Mallobaude, every Bretonnian duke alive at the time of the End Times, several famous Undead, a handful of Wood Elf heroes, and variants of the Green Knight and King Louen Leoncoeur reflecting their development during the campaign.

New Armies: The book includes combined army lists such as the Mousillon Army and the Alliance Against Undeath, together with restricted lists representing specific forces such as the Crusade Army and the Wildwood Host.

Narrative Scenarios: These scenarios recreate the campaign's defining battles in games of Warhammer.

How to Use These Rules

Like the official End Times books, this supplement is a toolbox from which you can select material for any game of Warhammer. Some scenarios require particular characters or units, but they can readily be adapted using different models or armies. The armies, characters, and units represent forces that fought during the End Times, but may also be used in other battles with your opponent's agreement.

We hope this supplement enriches your games of Warhammer.

Campaign Rules

The following campaign rules appear here rather than in the Game Guide because their effects depend on the rules system being used.

The Kingdom Without a King: The Loyalists are demoralised by the loss of the King. They suffer a -1 penalty to their Leadership for all Break tests.

Teeming Ranks: Mallobaude's legions are reinforced by the fresh corpses of the war's victims. A Mousillon army may include one unit of Zombies worth up to 200 points for each Necromancer or Master Necromancer in the army, including Arkhan and Kemmler.

Bretonnian Rules

The following Bretonnian terrain rules add character to the battlefield. They are taken from, or inspired by, Storm of Magic.

Chapel of the Lady
Many altars and chapels dedicated to the Lady stand upon the sites of heroic battles or over the graves of Grail Knights.

Building. A model belonging to the Chaos or Death alliance must re-roll successful ward saves while within 6" of an Altar or Chapel of the Lady. A Bretonnian unit occupying the Altar or Chapel has the Unbreakable special rule for as long as it remains there.

Pure Water Spring
The Lady of the Lake's sacred power can be felt in every clear pond and stream.

River. A Wizard within 6" of a Pure Water Spring may roll up to four dice when channelling instead of only one. If three or more of those dice roll a 6, immediately roll on the Miscast table. In addition, any unit within 3" of the spring has the Regeneration (5+) special rule.

For this campaign, every Damsel or Prophetess, including the Fay Enchantress, knows the following spell in addition to her normal spells.

Curse of the Lady - Cast on 12+

Casting aside the tattered remnants of virtue and nobility, the Damsel calls down the Lady's wrath upon those champions who dare oppose Bretonnia.

Hex. The spell targets a single enemy character within 12". The target must immediately pass a Leadership test with a -3 modifier or be transformed into a toad. Models belonging to the Chaos or Death alliance must re-roll successful tests, while Bretonnian models may re-roll failed tests.

While transformed, the model cannot channel or cast spells. Its magic items and other equipment have no effect, and all its characteristics are reduced to 1 except for its Wounds.

At the start of each of the controlling player's subsequent Magic phases, the player may roll a D6 for each transformed model. On a 4+, the spell ends and the model returns to its normal form. Mounts are unaffected.

Civil War

Note: These rules are adapted from the Civil War rules published in the French monthly edition of White Dwarf, issues 213-216, between January and April 2012. Only the rules needed for The Revolt of Mallobaude campaign are reproduced here.

For this campaign, ignore the Faction rule from the original expansion. It was intended to make armies more thematic, but does not suit the structure of this campaign.

In a Civil War, armies chosen from the same army book clash. Because both sides use many of the same rules, Jervis Johnson created a set of simple tables to add uncertainty and variety to the battle. These rules do not alter army selection, so no advance preparation is required.

Before the battle, roll once on two of the following tables. The first table is used by every army. The other two are specific to Bretonnia and Vampire Counts; the campaign or scenario rules specify which one to use.

Victor's Rewards

The winner of each battle chooses one of the following rewards. Rewards may be combined - for example, two Ancient Relics may be used in the same battle. Once used, a reward is spent and cannot be used again in a later battle.

Command from On High (Common): The supreme commander - Louen or Mallobaude - orders his forces to ignore a needless battle, leaving the enemy to find an empty field. Use this reward to avoid one battle at stage 2, 6, 8, or 10. Your opponent is treated as the victor and gains a reward, but you suffer no penalty for losing a Duke.

Challenge of Authority (Common): Use this reward when one of your characters is within 8" of an enemy character who has a Chivalric Virtue or Vampiric Power. Your character issues a resounding challenge, which the enemy must accept. From that point, either designated character may move in the Movement phase only if the move brings them closer together; any move that increases the distance between them is forbidden. Random Movement is unaffected. Once they are in base contact, the two characters are automatically placed in a challenge during the Close Combat phase, and neither may refuse. The challenger gains a +2 combat result bonus if they defeat their opponent.

Unexpected Reinforcements (Bretonnia): Prepare an additional unit worth no more than 150 points before the battle, but keep it secret. It may enter play from turn 3 onwards, from any table edge that touches your original deployment zone. If the deployment zone touches no table edge, as in a Last Stand, it may enter from any edge. The unit arrives after charges are declared and may make a full move.

Favour of the Lady (Bretonnia): For this battle, you have 1+D3 re-rolls. Each may be used on any dice roll, such as a Break test, To Hit roll, saving throw, or casting roll.

Ancient Relic (Common): Before deployment, give one of your characters a magic item worth up to 75 points at no cost.

Blood Kiss (Mousillon): A minor noble has received the Blood Kiss and joins the ranks of the accursed. At the start of the battle, choose one enemy champion or character with the Knight's Vow; named characters cannot be chosen. A champion is transformed into a Blood Knight champion on a 4+, a Hero into a Vampire Thrall on a 5+, or a Lord into a Vampire Lord on a 6+. If the roll fails, the victim resists the Kiss or overcomes the attacker. A transformed model retains all its magic equipment and receives an Undead equivalent of its original mount. It joins the Mousillon army as an independent character, even if it began the battle as a champion.

Nocturnal Summoning (Mousillon): The Necromancers have laboured through the night to draw fresh corpses from nearby graves. Immediately before the battle, add 2+D6 Skeletons or Zombies, or 1+D3 cavalry models, to an existing unit at no cost.

Nexus of Death (Mousillon): The Undead general has manoeuvred the foe into battle near a site that draws Death magic. Before deployment, the Rebel player may place two Sylvanian terrain features from Warhammer: Nagash - Book I in their half of the battlefield at no cost.

THE BRETONNIAN DUKES

Duke Armand of Aquitaine

Duke Armand of Aquitaine was one of the younger brothers of the previous Duke and he did not expect to inherit. As a Knight Errant, he was renowned for both his recklessness and his luck, and he refused all offers of a fiefdom until King Louen himself offered him a place in the royal household. Once again, Armand distinguished himself by his prowess, particularly by defeating the Bestigor Darmal the Hooked in single combat. Many expected him to be given a Barony and made a Marquess.

Instead, after just two years, he left on a Quest for the Grail. He left incognito, and did not speak of his activities after his return. A few minstrels attempted to gather information about his doings, but he was not the only Questing Knight to have concealed his identity. Upon his return, as a Grail Knight, he was appointed standard-bearer of Bretonnia, a position that suited him well.

In 2519, the old Duke of Aquitaine died in battle while intervening in one of the duchy's endless blood feuds. Four of his sons had died before him, and the youngest was simple-minded and was kept locked up. However, he also had two daughters and they would normally have inherited from him. The King chose to ignore them and grant the dukedom to Armand. He had to find his feet as an administrator; he was eager to solve problems personally and by force.

Grateful to the King, to whom he devoted absolute loyalty, Armand remained guided by his impulsiveness and by an intense sense of honour. It is these qualities and these faults that led him to face Mallobaude, and to meet on the battlefield the fate of all imprudent people.

Armand of Aquitaine is a Bretonnian Lord, who has taken the Grail Vow, endowed with the Virtue of Knightly Temper and riding a barded warhorse. You can choose his other options.

Duke Adalhard of Lyonesse

Duke Adalhard of Lyonesse was a simple man. He excelled on the battlefield, and off it, he enjoyed feasting, gambling, and other warrior pastimes. Some observers have suggested from his style that his true loyalty was to Ulric, not the Lady of the Lake. They put this fact down to the Norse blood which flows in the veins of the Lyonesses of the north.

The Duke stayed away from the politics of the duchy as much as possible. If a conflict grew too large for him to continue ignoring, he would bring in his troops, crush all opposing parties, seize their lands, and declare the matter resolved. This method proved effective every time he used it, and the nobles tried hard to plot without attracting the attention of their overlord.

After the disappearance of the Fay Enchantress Mallobaude's emissaries were quick to convince Adalhard that the Grail was only a lie. The Duke then joined the insurgents, and only recognised his error as he lay dying, after King Louen had mortally wounded him on the battlefield.

Adalhard of Lyonesse is a Bretonnian Lord, who has taken the Knight's Vow. His Norse blood gives him +1 in Weapon Skill, for an additional cost of +10 points. You can choose his other options.

Duke Tancred II of Quenelles

Duke Tancred II has proven himself to be an excellent lord for Quenelles. He had his moment of glory when he defeated the Necromancer Heinrich Kemmler and the Undead Chaos Champion Krell, who had allied with the Skaven and attacked Maisontaal Abbey. But it was in 1513 (2491 C.I.) and the Duke was at the time of the Mallobaude revolt an old man, stooped and diminished, although he still had good remnants of his former vigor.

Tancred, well aware that he was going to die, worried about the future of the duchy. He had had four sons, all dead or missing, and the Quenelles inheritance seemed very compromised. The final decision rested with the King, but Duke Tancred hoped to appoint a successor during his lifetime.

If he had paid attention to those around him, perhaps he would have noticed the value of his young cousin Jerrod. History later proved that the young man would become, much more than Tancred's heir, a true guide for the last Bretonnians still fighting to avoid the End Times.

Tancred II of Quenelles is a Bretonnian Lord who has taken the Grail Vow, and endowed with the Virtue of Purity. He always rides a barded warhorse and carries a cavalry lance. He always carries the following magic items, for an additional cost of +55 points. He cannot choose any other option.

Crown Blade: Magic Weapon. Against Undead, automatically wounds and cancels armour saves.

Grail Shield: Magic Armour. Shield. Any undead or Daemon in base-to-base contact loses an Attack.

Duke Alberic of Bordeleaux

Duke Alberic of Bordeleaux was famous for his personal courage and incredible self-discipline. We also know that he expected the Knights of his household to adhere to the same rigor and that he dismissed those who did not do so. As a result, his house was the smallest in strength among all the Dukes, but his Knights were among the best in the entire kingdom.

The Duke always wanted to go on the Grail Quest, but his father had died while he was wandering and Alberic never felt able to entrust responsibility for the duchy to anyone else. But with his eldest son, Frermund, having himself undertaken his own Grail Quest, the aging Alberic began looking for a steward to manage the duchy in his absence when the Civil War broke out.

Alberic de Bordeleaux is a Bretonnian Lord having taken the Knight's Vow and endowed with the Virtue of Discipline. He also has the special rule Self-discipline, all for an additional cost of +25 points. You may choose his other options.

Virtue of Discipline: The character and his unit are never disrupted when charged from the flank or rear.

Self-discipline: Knights of the Realm units must be promoted to Knights of Alberic for +3 points per model (+1 in Strength, or S4).

Duke Huebald of Carcassonne

Duke Huebald of Carcassonne was relatively small, thin and lively rather than powerful. He only spoke when it was necessary, and even then, remained as concise as possible. No one around him had ever seen him smile, let alone laugh. His union with his wife Schermilde was a political marriage and the fact that the couple had four children, all of whom took after the Duke, was a matter of astonishment. However, the Duke was respected by all his men. He was the best war leader the Gasconnais had had for generations and a courageous warrior to boot. Unlike many Bretonnian Knights, he was willing to use ambushes and feints to defeat his monstrous adversaries. He claimed that the greenskins who came down from the mountains to burn villages did not deserve to be fought honourably.

Although he used deception, Huebald could not stand it being used on him. Also when Mallobaude told him his “truth” about the Lady of the Lake, Huebald flew into a rage, matching Mallobaude’s fury when the latter had his revelation. The two men were subsequently closely linked, until the premature death of the Duke during the battle between him and the King's forces.

Huebald of Carcassonne is a Bretonnian Lord who took the Knight's Vow. Lively and Fast, he has a +1 bonus on his Initiative characteristic. He also has the Strategist special rule. All of these improvements have an additional cost of +15 points. You can choose his other options.

Strategist: An army regiment with a maximum of 200 points can benefit from the Ambush special rule.

Duke Bohemond of Bastonne

Duke Bohemond of Bastonne was a Grail Knight particularly famous for fighting monsters, so much so that he was nicknamed “the Bane of Beasts”. He was also known for refusing to fight inferior opponents, even if they attacked him. He would just knock them out and turn his attention elsewhere. When his fame grew, he traveled to many places in Bretonnia and beyond to find suitable adversaries.

Although Bohemond was a descendant of the line of Gilles le Breton, he showed absolute loyalty to King Louen and had no desire to seize the crown. In fact, he cared so little for power that he delegated the administration of his duchy to his Steward, and matters of justice to his Grand Justiciar. Unfortunately, he had little ability to judge the character of others and he had to replace these men at an alarming rate.

Bohemond of Bastonne is a Bretonnian Lord who has taken the Grail Vow, and endowed with the Virtue of the Impetuous Knight. He always rides a barded steed, and always carries the following magic items, for an additional cost of +95 points. He cannot choose any other option.

Bestial Mace of Bastonne: Magic Weapon. +2 Strength, multiple wounds (1D3).

Shield of Bohemond: Magic Armour. Shield. If Bohemond suffers at least one hit from a model possessing a magic weapon, the latter is destroyed on a roll of 4+ on 1D6 (make a single roll, regardless of the number of hits). An enemy whose magic weapon is destroyed counts as having a hand weapon. If the enemy has more than one magic weapon (a pair of weapons counts as a single weapon), roll 1D6 to see which one is destroyed.

Duke Hagen of Gisoreux

Duke Hagen of Gisoreux was a Grail Knight, close companion of King Louen. The Duke was entirely devoted to the cause of chivalry, even more so than most Grail Knights, and the King knew he could trust his advice on matters of chivalry. When he made a decision, Duke Hagen did not worry about the consequences and the King did not always follow his advice to the letter: he knew that sometimes it is better to forget chivalry when it comes to saving hundreds of lives.

The Duke sometimes had difficulty accepting these decisions. He knew well that the King had broader responsibilities and always had confidence in his friend's attachment to the ideals of chivalry. However, each time King Louen set aside these ideals for the common good, the Duke feared that he could be corrupted.

He settled in Couronne to spend most of his time there and could now easily manage the northern Gisoreux. The Duke's intransigence in matters of personal virtue did not help matters: he continued to rebel against practices that he considered unacceptable, but that the inhabitants of northern Gisoreux had perpetuated for generations. Duke Hagen rarely strayed from the King. The latter liked to have the Duke on hand to benefit from his advice, and the Duke, although he did not admit it to himself, wanted to keep an eye on his overlord.

After the defeat of Louen and the debacle of Quenelles, Hagen faced Mallobaude in single combat, but the vampire was too powerful, and the exemplary knight was slain.

Duke Folcard of Montfort

Duke Folcard of Montfort was something of an anomaly among the Dukes of Bretonnia. He was a formidable warrior, but he almost never fought on horseback. The mountains that cover his duchy seriously handicap mounted fighters, and the Duke could tolerate no delay in his fight against the Greenskins that had infested his lands before the arrival of Chaos.

Duke Folcard was perhaps the Bretonnian nobleman most beloved by his subjects. He seemed to sincerely care about the peasant villages and tried to help them when they were under attack from raiders. He set up a network of lanterns to communicate in the mountains, which allowed relief troops to arrive on time in most cases, and he intervene in person if he could. Once a village was liberated, he would hold an impromptu court on site to hear grievances about injustices and restore justice.

Folcard was a tall man, with dark hair cut very short, and a fierce and intense look. Many young peasant girls dreamed of their Duke, like most noble ladies. All mourned him when he was struck down by Mallobaude himself while defending his duchy.

Folcard of Montfort is a Bretonnian Lord who has taken the Knight's Vow, and is endowed with the Virtue of Empathy. You can choose his other options.

Duke Taubert de L’Anguille

Duke Taubert of L'Anguille was a sailor in his youth, but he neither set foot on a ship nor lived within sight of the sea for more than fifteen years after his last sea expedition. He confided the reasons to no one. After his return, Duke Taubert settled at Grasgar Castle, on the edge of the Forest of Arden, a castle that the Dukes had used as a hunting lodge for generations, but Duke Taubert had it enlarged and used it as a base for his expeditions into the heart of the forest. He claimed his goal was to defeat the Beastmen, but many believed he simply wanted to stay as far from the sea as possible.

Because of his absence, the Duke was unknown to most of the townspeople in the port of L'Anguille. Among the peasants in the countryside, things were very different. To them, the Duke was a hero who fought the Beastmen himself. He built a series of watchtowers all around the forest, which raised the alarm at Grasgar Castle when bands of raiders were spotted. Armed troops were then sent, often led by the Duke himself. More than one village was saved from absolute destruction by the timely arrival of the Duke's men, and he himself saved the lives of some of his subjects, which greatly improved their opinion of him.

In the years before the End Times, the Beastmen grew increasingly cunning, and the more brutal herds turned their attention to Lyonesse, Artois, and Couronne. This caused tensions with the surrounding duchies and some nobles muttered that Duke Taubert would have done better to defeat the Beastmen rather than drive them back to the lands of other lords.

Duke Cassyon of Parravon

Duke Cassyon of Parravon was the youngest Duke of Bretonnia at the time of the Civil War, being just over twenty years old. Regardless, he was a Grail Knight and he rode a Royal Pegasus. His father died while Cassyon was on his Grail Quest and the young Duke did not know of his status until returning to Parravon.

The Duke's advisors found him exasperating. He was an open and enthusiastic young man, who had no ill will towards anyone, which made him impossible to hate. He was quite gifted in this domain, but he would also have had to worry about taxes, justice and administration, because the Duke's neglect of the diplomatic responsibilities of his office gave rise to latent and growing resentment.

The prowess of Duke Cassyon had not escaped the Parravonese and rumors claimed that he was the reincarnation of Duke Agilgar.

Duke Chilfroy of Artois

Duke Chilfroy of Artois was an immense man, stronger and more robust than any of his Knights or nobles. He was also famous for his austerity: no one ever heard him laugh and it was claimed that he only smiled at the devastation he sowed among his enemies. While he wasn't very subtle, he was a great warlord when the strategies were simple and those who followed him only needed a little motivation.

The Duke spent most of his time hunting monsters, Beastmen and other creatures from the depths of the Forest of Arden. The great hall of the Château d'Artois was decorated with stuffed heads and furs from his greatest victories. This is where he held his court of justice. He was known for settling cases quickly by declaring the higher-ranking plaintiff in the right and meting out brutal punishment to the loser. As a result, Therefore, people did their best to avoid having to bring a case to the Duke, which suited Chilfroy just fine.

The Duke spent a lot of time riding deep in the forest, and there were rumors that he did not fight the Beastmen but made deals with them. Duke Theodoric of Brionne

Duke Theodoric of Brionne sometimes seemed to have a split personality. In battle, he was a true terror, swinging his great battle ax with deadly efficiency. Among his Knights, it was rumored that a spirit of war had taken possession of him, for he seemed to take great pleasure in massacres.

Far from the battlefield, the Duke was a protector of the arts, connoisseur of music and singing. He was the greatest patron of the minstrels of Bretonnia and listened attentively to their love stories.

At the time of Mallobaude's revolt, and for the months that followed, Theodoric remained mysteriously absent. Theodoric of Brionne is a Bretonnian Lord who has taken the Knight's Vow and carries a great weapon. He also has the special rule Frenzy, which adds an additional cost of +10 points. You may choose his other options.
