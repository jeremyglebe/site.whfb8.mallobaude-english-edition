Campaign stage 12 · the final siege

The Siege of Mousillon

Louen pursues the Serpent to his castle. Behind its walls, Mallobaude has replaced his unreliable followers with obedient dead.

Father and son at the last gate

Louen’s victory at Quenelles and the pursuit that followed have driven Mallobaude back to Mousillon. The king now comes to end his illegitimate son’s rebellion at its source. Unlike the route to Couronne, Louen is still present to lead the final assault, and his survival will shape the kingdom that emerges from it.

Within the walls, Mallobaude has discarded the last pretence that his followers share in his cause. He has the surviving living nobles and knights killed and raised, and gives the peasants to his ghouls. The would-be liberator’s stronghold has become a prison of the dead. Arkhan, Kemmler and Krell do not take part in this final defence: the confrontation is between Louen’s army and Mallobaude’s Vampire Counts.

The Serpent's last refuge

Arrive when Mousillon is selected during The Flight of the Serpent, or after skipping that optional stage. Carry forward the campaign record and the fate of the surviving leaders.

Mallobaude reaches his castle and bargains with the creatures lurking beneath it. Blaming human weakness for his defeats, he feeds most of his remaining peasants to the ghouls and executes the nobles and knights, raising them again as perfectly obedient servants. He fortifies the castle for a siege he hopes can last for years.

A few peasants escape and tell Louen what awaits him. The king knows that prolonged bombardment will achieve little against corpses the necromancers can restore. Once the principal defences are damaged, he mounts his hippogryph and orders the assault.

The account sets the scene. Use the siege procedure and the army requirements below to play the battle.

Assault the castle

The battlefield represents the approaches to Castle Mousillon. A fortress is required. Arrange it using the siege procedure in Blood in the Badlands.

  1. Deploy the Loyalist army first.
  2. Deploy the entire Rebel army afterward, subject to the relief-force agreement.
  3. The Loyalists take the first turn.

After deployment, roll a D6 for each necromancer. On a 6, remove it as a casualty: the deserters rule continues from the pursuit. Arkhan and Kemmler are prohibited here, so their usual exemptions do not apply.

Play six game turns, unless an immediate victory ends the battle.

No measured deployment diagram is supplied for this scenario. The illustration on the scenario page is a map of Mousillon, not a measured battle deployment plan.

Siege procedure

Prepare the fortress

The Loyalists attack; the Rebels defend. Each player has a separate siege-equipment allowance equal to their army's points value. For example, a 2,000-point army receives another 2,000 siege points. Buy from your side's list below. Items may be bought more than once within that allowance.

  1. The Rebel player arranges the fortress. It may have any configuration supported by the available sections; one table edge belongs to the defenders, so a complete perimeter is unnecessary. The Loyalist player sets up the terrain outside it using a normal battlefield-generation method.
  2. Place three objectives on three different wall, gate or tower sections. The Loyalist player places the first, the Rebel player the second, and the Loyalist player the third. Their exact position within a section does not matter.
  3. Deploy the Loyalists outside the fortress, more than 8" from its walls. Then deploy the entire Rebel army inside the fortress and on its walls, subject to your agreed relief-force ruling below. This scenario reverses the usual siege deployment order.
  4. During deployment, defending units may split to occupy several sections. Each part becomes an independent unit for the rest of the battle and cannot rejoin the others. Distribute or split command groups among these parts as desired.

Do not make starvation rolls. After both armies have deployed, resolve the pre-battle phase below. The Loyalists then take the first turn. Play six game turns unless an immediate victory ends the battle.

Agree before selecting forces: decide whether this scenario's deployment of the entire Rebel army removes the normal relief force. If you retain relief, divide the Rebel army into a defence force and a reserve relief force, each containing at least one non-character unit. There is no prescribed points ratio. Use the arrival and relief-victory rules below only if you retain that reserve. Record the decision before buying siege equipment.

The pre-battle phase

Resolve these steps in order, once, after deployment and before turn one.

  1. Defensive artillery: roll two dice for each defending stone thrower or cannon in the fortress. Each 6 destroys one attacking war machine within that defending machine's range and line of sight. Add any dice bought through Sally Forth!
  2. Attacking artillery: roll one die for each attacking stone thrower, cannon or bolt thrower. For each 6, roll again: 1–3 destroys one defending war machine within the attacking machine's range and line of sight; 4–6 breaches one wall section within its range and line of sight. A bolt thrower's second roll of 4–6 misses instead. Add the dice bought through Undermines, which can only cause breaches.
  3. Defensive missile fire: defending troops that did not shoot in the first step may now shoot twice. If artillery misfires in this step, do not roll on its misfire table; it cannot be used in the first battle turn.
  4. The assault begins: start the Loyalist first turn.

Moving and shooting within the fortress

Use the normal building rules with the following changes. Treat every wall, gate and tower section as a separate building. Any unit may garrison a fortress section, regardless of troop type.

  • A unit may move into a connected, unoccupied section. Friendly units in adjacent sections may exchange places.
  • A unit may assault an enemy-held connected section in the Combat phase. If it wins, it captures that section; if it loses, it remains where it was.
  • A tower or gate section may hold one war machine as well as a garrison. Put the machine on top and measure its attacks from there. Enemy missile fire may target either the machine or the unit. Crew may be selected to defend an assault. Capturing the section destroys its war machine.
  • Units may pass through gates held by their own side as through clear ground. They may declare charges at enemies on the other side as though they could see them.
  • Up to ten models, plus a war machine, may shoot from each section.

Assaulting a section

Outside attackers have ladders or grappling hooks. Unless they fly, halve their Weapon Skill and Initiative, rounding down. Attacks from connected sections, from siege towers, or into breached sections do not suffer this penalty.

One unit outside the fortress may assault a section, together with one enemy unit from each connected section. At most ten models per side fight in the assault. When several attacking units participate, choose their ten combatants from any of those units. The defender may also select combatants from a friendly unit in a connected section.

The defending garrison adds +1 combat resolution for each connected section occupied by a friendly unit. Other combat-resolution bonuses remain excluded under the normal building-assault rules.

Rocks

Every garrison has rocks in addition to its usual weapons: range 1", Strength 3, Quick to Fire. Rocks always hit on 3+, ignoring Ballistic Skill and normal to-hit modifiers. They may be used to stand and shoot against an assault, including by Undead units and war-machine crews. They cannot be used for that reaction against flyers or an assault from a connected section or siege tower.

Breaches

Mark breached sections. If an attacker wins a combat against a breached section, the defender must leave even if it does not break. Place it outside using the normal rules for abandoning a building, or in an adjacent unoccupied fortress section. If it cannot leave, destroy it. The attacking unit occupies the section.

Fortress sections are never destroyed or removed. A spell, magic item or special rule that would remove a building instead breaches the affected fortress section.

Relief force, if retained

From Rebel turn two onward, roll 2D6 at the start of each Rebel turn and add that turn's number. A total of 10 or more brings on the entire relief force. Hasty Assault changes this schedule as described below.

Roll a D6 for its entry edge: 1–2, the edge to the left of the fortress; 3–4, the opposite edge; 5–6, the edge to its right. Bring the units on using the core Reinforcements rule, page 27. Reinforcements arrive in Remaining Moves, facing inward with their rear rank touching the entry edge; each model must remain within twice its Movement allowance of that edge. They cannot charge or march on arrival and count as having moved, but may otherwise act normally.

Relief units cannot enter or attack fortress sections from outside: they lack ladders. Once inside the fortress, they may move into or attack its sections normally.

Relieved! The Rebels win immediately if a relief unit of at least five models enters the fortress through a gateway before the end of turn five. A defending unit must occupy the gate to open it. Flying, tunnelling or another route into the courtyard does not qualify.

Relief deadline: the printed relief victory uses turn five. Mousillon extends the battle to six turns without expressly changing that deadline. If retaining relief, record before play whether you keep turn five or agree an extension; do not assume it moves automatically.

Loyalist siege equipment

Siege Tower — 1,000 siege points

A siege tower is a movable building, occupied using the normal building rules. Before moving it, roll a D6: on 1 it cannot move that turn; on 2–6 it moves 8". It cannot charge, wheel or reform, and always moves straight forward, but may contact a fortress section. Its occupants may then assault as though they had charged.

Once it contacts the fortress, the tower becomes a connected fortress section for the rest of the battle. It cannot move again or be driven back when its occupants lose combat. Treat it as part of the fortress; defenders may assault and capture it.

Battering Ram — 500 siege points

The ram is an Unbreakable chariot. Its profile is M 1D6+2, T 6, W 5; its other characteristics are dashes. Its five crew have WS 3, BS 3, S 3, I 3, A 1 and Ld 7; their M, T and W entries are dashes. The ram cannot charge, wheel or reform: move it straight forward, allowing contact with fortress sections.

At the start of each Combat phase while it contacts a section, roll a D6. On 5+ the section is breached; add +1 when battering a gate. The ram may batter a section while another unit assaults it.

Monstrous Battering Clubs — 250 siege points per unit

Available to a monster or a unit of monstrous infantry. Within 1" of a fortress section, it may batter instead of making its normal attacks. Roll at the start of each Combat phase: 5+ breaches the section, adding +1 against a gate.

Siege Ammunition — 100 siege points per unit

Available to stone throwers and cannons. When an equipped machine hits a fortress section in a normal Shooting phase, roll a D6: 6+ breaches it. This does not apply in the pre-battle phase.

Hasty Assault — half the starting siege-point allowance

If a relief force is retained, it cannot start rolling to arrive until Rebel turn four and suffers −1 to each arrival roll. The item's normal −1 modifier to starvation rolls has no effect here because this scenario has no starvation rolls.

Undermines — 250 siege points

Each purchase adds two dice to the attacking-artillery pre-battle step. A 6 still requires the second roll: 4–6 breaches a wall, while 1–3 has no effect. Undermines cannot destroy defending war machines.

Rebel siege equipment

Haunted Mansion — 500 siege points

This tower upgrade is available to Forces of Destruction. A unit occupying the tower causes Fear. Before combat, each enemy unit in base contact with the tower suffers D6 Strength 1 hits with no armour saves; these count towards combat resolution. Only one Tower Upgrade may be applied to a tower section.

Witches Cauldron — 500 siege points

A unit and/or character in the cauldron's section may drink at the start of any of its turns. Roll a D6: on 1, the unit suffers D6 Strength 3 hits with no armour saves; on 2–6, one randomly selected characteristic increases by +1 until the start of the unit's next turn.

Flaming Ammunition — 100 siege points per unit

Available to a defending unit with a missile weapon, including a war machine. Once per battle, instead of its normal attack, it may attack an enemy war machine in range, including a siege tower or battering ram. Roll one D6 for the entire attack regardless of the unit's size: on 5+ the target is destroyed.

Cauldron of Boiling Oil — 250 siege points per unit

Once per battle, place the small round template within 3" of the unit. Every model under it suffers a Strength 4 hit with Armour Piercing. Agree its activation timing before play.

Warded Tower — 250 siege points

Any unit occupying the chosen tower section has Magic Resistance (2).

Hell Gate — 250 siege points

The gate has a Strength 4 Breath Weapon, usable in the Combat phase against units attacking its section. Apply the normal Breath Weapon rules.

Sally Forth! — 250 siege points

Each purchase adds two dice to the defensive-artillery pre-battle step. It may destroy attacking war machines, including siege towers and battering rams.

Equipment decisions: before buying the affected items, agree how Undermines and Sally Forth! choose targets and determine range or line of sight without a firing machine. Also agree the Witches Cauldron's random-characteristic method and the Boiling Oil activation timing.

Ordinary siege result

If no immediate victory has ended the battle, count the objectives controlled by the Loyalist attackers at the end of turn six: all three gives a Loyalist victory; zero or one gives a Rebel victory; exactly two requires ordinary victory points to determine the winner. Apply the campaign-ending agreement recorded before play if both leaders survive.

For ordinary victory points, score the full cost of enemy units destroyed or fled from the table. Add 100 for an enemy general slain or fled from the table, 25 per captured standard or 100 for the battle standard, and 50 when a unit champion kills an enemy character in a challenge. A standard qualifies when removed through Last Stand or slain in close combat, not through shooting, magic or another non-combat loss. Both a character and its mount must be destroyed to claim their points. Created units normally award none.

Wiping out the opposing army or leading by at least 100 victory points wins; otherwise the ordinary result is a draw. A points victory is crushing if the winner also scores at least twice the opponent's total. These points rules are needed only when the attackers control exactly two objectives.

Siege rules: Blood in the Badlands, printed pages 88–94. This procedure applies Mousillon's deployment order, first turn, six-turn duration and exemption from starvation. The leader-death rule and agreed campaign ending still apply.

Win the battle

If Mallobaude or Louen dies, stop the game immediately: his opponent wins. If neither dies, the scenario tells you to determine the winner using ordinary siege rules.

Settle before play: the campaign guide instead says that if both leaders survive, the campaign ends in a draw and the players write their own conclusion. Agree how to handle a siege-rules victory with both alive. Also agree what happens if both die simultaneously. Full wording and unresolved cases.
