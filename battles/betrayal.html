From Châlons to open civil war

Finish Châlons first. Bring three separate facts into this stage: whether Mallobaude possesses the Companions' relics, whether he won Châlons, and whether Drycha abducted Morgiana. Also bring your updated duke roster, including deaths and earlier defections. Do not replace those played outcomes with the narrative's historical result.

The crown answers

In the campaign's account, Carcassonne, Lyonesse and Artois declare for Mallobaude after Châlons. Rebellion gives way to civil war. The traitors fight fiercely, but Louen's loyal knights initially gain the upper hand under the Lady's protection.

Louen strikes first at nearby Artois. His spies have watched Chilfroy gathering men and mounts in the forest; when the betrayal becomes open, they sabotage the duke's war machines. The royal lances break the rebels. In Lyonesse, Louen then faces Adalhard, a former friend deceived by Mallobaude. Mortally wounded by the king, Adalhard recognises his error and asks the Lady's forgiveness. Louen grants him the funeral honours of a Grail Knight.

In Carcassonne, Tancred II carries out the king's justice. His duel with Huebald is fought in words as well as blows; when doubt distracts the rebel duke, Tancred's sword ends it. After a year of campaigning, the rebel duchies seem restored and Mallobaude's cause appears spent.

That is the source's account, not a compulsory result. It supplies no special sabotage procedure, forced duel, or predetermined Loyalist victory for these games. Use the battles and consequences below.

The Serpent's influence

Check each condition independently. Each satisfied condition brings one duke over to Mallobaude:

  1. He possesses the Companions' relics: one duke whose loyalty is not absolute defects.
  2. He won Châlons and defeated Armand: a second duke joins him.
  3. Drycha abducted Morgiana, the Lady's representative: a third joins him.

If Morgiana was abducted but subsequently recovered, the source does not explicitly state whether this condition is fulfilled. Carry forward the interpretation agreed when resolving Châlons.

Give priority to Adalhard of Lyonesse, Chilfroy of Artois and Huebald of Carcassonne. If they are already dead or converted, select replacements from dukes whose loyalty is not absolute, using the allegiance roster. Do not convert an absolutely loyal duke or revive a dead one to fill the quota.

The source prescribes one to three battles, but the conditions can yield no new defector. It does not specify a fallback if no eligible rebel duke remains, or if too few corruptible dukes are available. Keep the actual campaign record and agree how to proceed rather than inventing a defection.

Fight the royal response

Play one to three Pitched Battles. Alternatively, the source explicitly allows one battle featuring all the corrupted dukes together.

  • The Loyalist army must include at least one loyal duke, or King Louen himself.
  • The Rebel army must include one of the renegade dukes. Mallobaude may not take part.
  • For the combined-battle option, include all the corrupted dukes rather than silently choosing just one.

Use the ordinary Bretonnian stage of the campaign, with the Civil War tables below. Character profiles remain in the rules reference and the army books. Mallobaude's Revelation is not available until the next stage.

As in The Revolt, this entry uses Battleline as an explicit editorial interpretation of the source's generic Pitched Battle. No special points ratio, deployment map or sabotage rule is supplied for this stage.

Ordinary Battleline setup

  1. Agree equal points. Place at least D6+4 terrain pieces, alternating placement after a roll-off.
  2. Roll off for choice of table half. Deploy in opposing zones more than 12″ from the centre line.
  3. Roll off separately for first placement and alternate deploying units. All war machines form one placement; all characters deploy last as one placement.
  4. Roll off for first turn, adding +1 for the player who finished deployment first.
  5. Play six game turns or an agreed time limit, unless a Civil War result changes this.

Core victory-point procedure

Score the full cost of enemy units destroyed or fled from the table. Add 100 points if the enemy general is slain or has fled the table, 25 per captured standard or 100 for the battle standard, and 50 when a unit champion kills an enemy character in a challenge. A standard qualifies when removed through Last Stand or slain in close combat, not through shooting, magic or another non-combat loss. Both a character and its mount must be destroyed to claim their points. Created units normally award none.

Apply the official v1.8 correction: wiping out the opposing army or leading by at least 100 victory points wins. A points victory is crushing if the winner also scores at least twice the opponent's total. Otherwise the result is a draw. Scenario rules and applicable Civil War results can replace ordinary duration or victory conditions.

Source: core rulebook, printed pp. 142–144, with official update v1.8, PDF pp. 2 and 6. This includes the corrected victory threshold, not the obsolete threshold in the uncorrected scan.

Civil War procedure and tables

Note: These rules are adapted from the Civil War rules published in the French monthly edition of White Dwarf, issues 213-216, between January and April 2012. Only the rules needed for The Revolt of Mallobaude campaign are reproduced here.

For this campaign, ignore the Faction rule from the original expansion. It was intended to make armies more thematic, but does not suit the structure of this campaign.

In a Civil War, armies chosen from the same army book clash. Because both sides use many of the same rules, Jervis Johnson created a set of simple tables to add uncertainty and variety to the battle. These rules do not alter army selection, so no advance preparation is required.

Before the battle, roll once on two of the following tables. The first table is used by every army. The other two are specific to Bretonnia and Vampire Counts; the campaign or scenario rules specify which one to use.

2D6Civil War Battle Table
2

Annihilation

The only way this dispute will be resolved is by the complete destruction of the other side.

All units in both armies are Unbreakable. Ignore the normal rules for Game Length and Victory Conditions. Instead, the game lasts until one side is completely destroyed; the surviving side wins.

3

Lust for Battle

The troops in both armies are desperate to get to grips with each other.

All units in both armies have the Vanguard special rule.

4

Pent-Up Fury

The two sides are seething with pent-up aggression.

All units in both armies have the Devastating Charge special rule.

5

Undeserving

Each side considers the other unworthy to carry the battle standards under which they march.

The Victory Points for captured standards increase to 100 points per standard bearer and 500 points for a Battle Standard Bearer.

6

We Will Not Yield

Neither side is willing to back away from this fight.

All units in both armies are Stubborn.

7

Ancient Enmity

The two sides have harboured numerous grudges against each other for centuries.

Roll twice on this table, re-rolling any further rolls of 7 or duplicate results.

8

Hated Rivals

The two generals despise each other.

Each Army General Hates the opposing Army General and wins the battle immediately if that rival is slain. If both Generals are slain at exactly the same time, the battle is a draw.

9

Bloody Feud

The two sides have been feuding for years.

All units in both armies Hate all units in the other army.

10

Unreasoning Spite

Both sides are so blinded by hate that they have lost all reason.

All units in both armies are subject to Frenzy.

11

We Shall Not Yield

Both rival armies believe completely in the justness of their cause.

All units in both armies are Immune to Psychology.

12

Guerrilla War

The two sides have been ravaging each other's lands in a vicious guerrilla war.

Up to three units in each army may have the Ambushers special rule if their commander wishes.

2D6Bretonnian Civil War Table
2

Favours of the Lady

Will the Lady favour her sons?

The first time a unit is called upon to make a ward save granted by the Blessing of the Lady, it must also take a Leadership test. If the test is passed, all ward saves the unit receives from the Blessing are improved by 1.

3

Thoroughbreds

All Warhorses, Pegasi, and Royal Pegasi have +1 Strength.

4

As a Token of My Esteem

A handkerchief, scarf, or ribbon tied around a knight's lance or armour may have miraculous effects.

Each player may choose one magic item from the Bretonnian magic items list and give it to a Hero or Lord in their army, following the normal rules for magic items. If the bearer is slain in close combat by another character, the slayer automatically takes the item.

5

The True King

Claimants to the throne are all too common, and only the army's great standard proves the General's right to command.

If the Battle Standard Bearer is slain, every unit in that army must take a Panic test.

6

Duel to the Death

Before the battle, a hero from each army is chosen to represent their side in a duel of honour.

Before the game begins, each player nominates one Lord or Hero. Place the two characters in base contact at the centre of the battlefield; they fight normally in the next Close Combat phase. No other model may interfere, and both characters are Unbreakable. The victor causes Terror for the rest of the battle.

7

Ancient Enmity

The two sides have harboured numerous grudges against each other for centuries.

Roll twice on this table, re-rolling any further rolls of 7 or duplicate results.

8

Valour

Every knight is determined to enter into legend.

Whenever a character slays an enemy in a challenge, compare their points values. If the slain model was worth more points than the victor, the victor's side scores 100 Victory Points. If the battle does not use Victory Points, take comfort that the hero's deed will be celebrated throughout the land!

9

The Jacquerie

The peasants rise in revolt and refuse to bend to the nobility's will.

Units with the Peasant's Duty special rule cannot use the Leadership of a nearby knight, but gain Frenzy and Stubborn.

10

For the Glory of the Damsels

Magic floods the realm, invigorating the powers of its Damsels and Prophetesses.

Whenever a Damsel or Prophetess casts a spell, add D6 to the casting total. This additional die can cause a miscast as normal.

11

Lances of Heroes

The blood of Gilles le Breton runs in the veins of his sons!

Knights of any type re-roll To Wound rolls of 1 in a turn in which they charge.

12

The Grail

Place a marker representing the Grail at the centre of the battlefield. A Knight unit or character with any vow other than the Grail Vow that moves into contact with the marker must stop and drink. Take a Leadership test: if failed, the unit or character has Stupidity for the rest of the battle; if passed, replace its current vow with the Grail Vow. Only one unit or character may drink each turn. If an enemy unit is in contact with the Grail, it must first be destroyed or made to flee.

Record each duke's fate

  • Rebel defeat: the renegade duke is killed or imprisoned and cannot take part in any further End Times campaign.
  • Rebel victory: the duke remains with the insurgents.

The source does not prescribe a separate consequence for a draw. Its optional combined battle also does not explicitly restate how the singular “the duke” consequence applies to several participating dukes. Decide those interpretations openly rather than treating a new ruling as translated text.

Guide p.16 does not explicitly award a Victor's Reward in this stage, unlike the adjacent stages. The general reward rules remain at Rules p.11; do not silently treat the omission as a new stage-specific reward grant.

Once all these battles are complete, continue to Stage 5: The Conquest. Carry forward which dukes remain rebel, which are loyal, and which are unavailable.
