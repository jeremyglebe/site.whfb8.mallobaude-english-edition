Battles: Sources and Rules Notes

References and unresolved interpretations for the combined campaign scenarios. The battle pages retain short prompts for rulings needed before play; their detailed basis is recorded here.

Quenelles

Campaign paths and consequences: French Guide v1.8 pp.18,20–21. Scenario: Rules v1.5 pp.49–51, by Ilmarith and Thindaraïel with Dreadaxe; campaign penalties: p.6. The guide credits its narrative to the eighth-edition Wood Elves book and Warhammer: Nagash.

Core rounding: printed p.7. Reinforcements: p.27, with official v1.8 amendment. These were checked against original scans. Army lists and character profiles remain linked dependencies. No source map exists for these battles, and none has been invented.

At the forest's edge Ariel collapses, stricken by a mysterious poison. Orion forgets his enemies and rushes to help her, and the Asrai withdraw. This is the source's historical account, not a required Loyalist defeat: your battle result decides the campaign route below. If Morgiana remains uncaptured in your campaign, do not overwrite that fact with the historical account's assumption that she has disappeared.

Agree before play: Rules p.51 explicitly lists Civil War (Bretonnia), whereas Guide p.18 says to stop using it after Revelation. The sources conflict for the revealed route. The tables are included below, but this page does not silently choose which instruction overrides the other.

Use Pitched Battle setup on identical tables, adding more forests to Ariel's deployment half near Athel Loren. No measured map, forest count or table dimensions are provided. The Battleline setup below is our labelled practical interpretation of Pitched Battle.

Record each selected unit's originating flank, surviving models and remaining wounds. The source does not specify selection after a drawn flank or how attached characters count toward this quota; do not add an invented extra character allowance.

Unresolved in the French: “defeated” is not defined here as slain, breaking or losing a combat round. Agree the trigger rather than silently changing it to “killed.” The Louen passage specifies Bretonnians; it does not extend Panic to the Asrai or mean automatic flight.

The scenario's “Instability test” has no verified procedure. Core Unstable inflicts wounds without a test; Daemonic Instability is not an appropriate substitution. Vampire Counts' Slain General procedure is a possible analogy but has different timing and exceptions, so it is not presented as the rule here. A revealed-route game needs an explicit agreed ruling before play.

The source supplies no campaign continuation for a drawn decisive battle. Do not force the historical outcome as a tie-break.

For the rest of the campaign, every Mousillon army gains Plentiful Ranks: it may add one Zombie unit worth at most 200 points per Necromancer or Master Necromancer, including Arkhan and Kemmler. The source does not explicitly settle payment or army-selection accounting; this entry does not silently label those units free.

Conquest

Consolidated adaptation of French Guide v1.8 pp.17–20, with the corruption roster on p.14. The Conquest narrative is credited to Thindaraïel; Revelation draws on Warhammer: Nagash and The Return of Nagash; the Asrai account draws on the eighth-edition Wood Elves army book.

Campaign procedures are gathered here; full army lists, character profiles, rewards and the Red Duke battle remain linked dependencies. No later End Times battle or unsupported alternate route has been added.

The first-target restrictions and later-target restrictions differ in the French text. They have not been silently made identical. A draw leaves no winner to choose the next target; the source does not supply a tie procedure.

Play a Pitched Battle. The ordinary Battleline procedure below is the same clearly labelled editorial interpretation used in the earlier engagements, not an extra named scenario imposed by the French. Before Revelation, use the Bretonnian Civil War tables below; after Revelation, do not use them.

From the beginning of this stage, Mallobaude may reveal his corruption. This is a change in campaign state, not a compulsory result of the first battle. In the source narrative, Arkhan arranged the Blood Kiss for Mallobaude; as his human allies fell, the dead filled their ranks. The civil war provided Arkhan with more bodies than even he had expected. Kemmler and Krell reinforced the rebellion, although Kemmler's ambitions made him a less dependable ally than Krell.

The source does not specify a mid-battle timing window for Revelation or how already-earned Bretonnian rewards are converted. Do not invent an in-game transformation or exchange rate. Record when Revelation occurs and preserve any agreed interpretation.

Use the reward rules and the appropriate army's reward categories, including the change to Rebel categories after Revelation. The source does not explain how the singular duke consequence applies if several eligible dukes are present; do not silently extend it to everyone.

Road To Couronne

Scenario sequence and narrative: French Guide v1.8 p.22, credited to Warhammer: Nagash. Persistent consequences: Guide p.21 and Rules p.6; duke corruption: Guide p.14. Core procedure uses the verified rulebook and official update cited above.

This is a consolidated adaptation, not an alteration of the faithful translation. The optional-stage routing ambiguity, army accounting and unavailable required dukes have not been silently repaired.

In the source's account, no one can agree on Louen's fate. Some say seven sisters carried him to Silverspire for the Lady to heal; others say he lies buried above Quenelles. Worse rumours make him a puppet of Mallobaude's necromancers. These remain rumours, not additional campaign rules. Without their king, the dukes hesitate to help their neighbours while their own lands burn. Southern duchies fall one by one and Mallobaude marches north.

The Serpent's confidence becomes recklessness. At Gisoreux, Adelaix, Montfort and many other places, he challenges every knight willing to face him and emerges unharmed in the historical account. Those victories are not imposed on your games.

This stage is optional. If skipped, count Mallobaude as having won one victory, then proceed onward. The source says “the next stage” without reconciling this with Maisontaal's optional Parravon requirement; agree the route rather than silently awarding a conquest or compulsory detour.

When Couronne is chosen, proceed to The Battle of Couronne. A crushing Loyalist victory forces that choice as described below. The page's first-target exclusion and later Couronne route are both preserved; do not read the initial prohibition as making the finale impossible.

No special points ratio, deployment or first-turn exception is supplied for this stage. As in the earlier engagements, Battleline is an explicit editorial interpretation of the generic Pitched Battle instruction.

Every Mousillon army may add one Zombie unit worth at most 200 points per Necromancer or Master Necromancer, including Arkhan and Kemmler. The text does not expressly settle payment or army-selection accounting; this page does not silently call the units free.

Year Of Woe

This page is a consolidated adaptation for use at the table, not the literal translation. It reorganizes verified material, combines context with procedure, and labels examples and unresolved questions. No new scenario rule or branch has been adopted.

  • Triumph & Treachery, printed pp. 16–17; Glottkin, Book II, printed pp. 12–13 and 20; Blood in the Badlands, printed pp. 89–91. Procedures summarized from verified original scans, not additional scenarios imported wholesale.
  • English Guide p. 12 and French Guide v1.8 p. 12: narrative, optional stage, reward and onward path.
  • English Rules pp. 45–46 and French Rules v1.5 pp. 45–46: scenario by Ilmarith, assisted by Dreadaxe and Thindaraïel.
  • Rules p. 11: Victor's Rewards and Ancient Relic. Check this linked reward section when preparing campaign records.

The guide credits its background to Warhammer: Nagash and Warhammer Armies: Daemons of Chaos. Those references do not make this fan-authored battle an official End Times scenario. Official scenarios and the transitions into them will be added only after their source rules and campaign dependencies are checked.

In the source narrative, Ku'gath strikes down Kairos, then falls to some ten blessed lances. Both Daemonic armies vanish, leaving the relics to Mallobaude. He displays them to lend legitimacy to his bid for power. That is the narrative's result, not a compulsory outcome for your battle: use the victory conditions below.

For example, an agreed value of 3,000 points gives Mallobaude 1,500 and each Daemon army 3,000. This is an arithmetic example, not a recommended or required battle size. Normal army selection restrictions still need to be checked.

Source ambiguity: the diagram allows play without a fortress, while the deployment text requires war machines inside one. The source does not explain how to reconcile those instructions. No substitute deployment ruling has been silently added to this draft.

Unresolved source interaction: Glottkin grants the spell to all Chaos Wizards, but this scenario restricts Ku'gath's army selection to Nurgle. It does not explain how that selection restriction interacts with summoning Tzeentch models during play. No exemption has been invented here.

These are the referenced splitting, movement, shooting and assault procedures, not the complete Badlands siege campaign. Do not add starvation, relief forces, preliminary bombardment, siege budgets or its five-turn victory conditions. The sources do not explicitly settle passage through an open, enemy-held gate.

Reward interaction to verify: the general reward table awards a reward to each battle winner, while this stage specifies four Ancient Relics. This draft does not silently add a fifth reward or decide whether a Daemon victor receives a campaign reward.

The guide specifies no relic award for the Rebel player and still directs the campaign to the Revolt. It does not supply a new narrative for Mallobaude's survival or the fate of a slain general. Record the result rather than inventing a resurrection or new branch as an official rule.

This summarizes Triumph & Treachery, printed pp. 16–17. The official heading is Trust No One. Victory Tokens, Treachery Cards and Mercenaries are separate modules: they are not silently imported here. This scenario supplies its own scoring.

Chalons

  • French Guide pp. 13–16: route into Châlons, narrative, aftermath and next stage.
  • French Rules pp. 47–48: scenario by Ilmarith and Thindaraïel, assisted by Dreadaxe.
  • Rules pp. 7–9: the campaign's Civil War procedure and Bretonnian tables, not a substitution from another edition.

This is an editorial consolidation of a fan-authored battle. The fan guide credits and adapts official Wood Elf background; that does not make this battle an official Games Workshop scenario. No new rescue, resurrection, capture ruling or End Times transition has been invented.

The Revolt is optional. If skipped, the guide says to count Mallobaude as having won one battle before moving to this stage. Bring forward your actual record of rewards, conquered dukedoms and dukes' loyalties, rather than assuming that every game followed the narrative.

The Wildwood Host emerges as an apparent ally of the Loyalists, but its purpose is not Armand's victory. Drycha intends to seize Morgiana. The source story carries the Fay into Mannfred's hands and ultimately into the preparations for Nagash's return; that future is not forced upon your game.

The 100% Rebel budget is the common agreed value; the source explicitly scales the other two forces to 75% and 50%. Character profiles and the full Host list remain linked references in this draft.

The French source uses “less than 3″” for the abduction and her return, and a maximum of 3″ for the Host's arrival. Those distinctions are retained here.

Apply the Civil War rules to the Bretonnian forces only. The forest Spirits are unaffected. The source-compared common procedure and the relevant tables are reproduced below so the scenario does not send you to another chapter for them.

Châlons changes army budgets, deployment, turn order and arrivals: do not replace these with Battleline setup. The source does not settle victory-point attribution when Wildwood units change sides.

Betrayal

This is a consolidated adaptation, separate from the faithful translation. Narrative and campaign procedure: French Guide v1.8 p.16, with the eligibility roster on p.14 and Revelation timing on pp.17–18. The guide credits its account to Warhammer: Nagash, supplemented by Thindaraïel. Civil War: Rules pp.7–9. Core procedure and scoring use the verified rulebook and official update cited above.

No historical duke's death, sabotage effect, or unprinted alternate branch has been made into a compulsory rule. Full army profiles remain external dependencies.

Finish Châlons first. Bring three separate facts into this stage: whether Mallobaude possesses the Companions' relics, whether he won Châlons, and whether Drycha abducted Morgiana. Also bring your updated duke roster, including deaths and earlier defections. Do not replace those played outcomes with the narrative's historical result.

That is the source's account, not a compulsory result. It supplies no special sabotage procedure, forced duel, or predetermined Loyalist victory for these games. Use the battles and consequences below.

If Morgiana was abducted but subsequently recovered, the source does not explicitly state whether this condition is fulfilled. Carry forward the interpretation agreed when resolving Châlons.

The source prescribes one to three battles, but the conditions can yield no new defector. It does not specify a fallback if no eligible rebel duke remains, or if too few corruptible dukes are available. Keep the actual campaign record and agree how to proceed rather than inventing a defection.

As in The Revolt, this entry uses Battleline as an explicit editorial interpretation of the source's generic Pitched Battle. No special points ratio, deployment map or sabotage rule is supplied for this stage.

The source does not prescribe a separate consequence for a draw. Its optional combined battle also does not explicitly restate how the singular “the duke” consequence applies to several participating dukes. Decide those interpretations openly rather than treating a new ruling as translated text.

Guide p.16 does not explicitly award a Victor's Reward in this stage, unlike the adjacent stages. The general reward rules remain at Rules p.11; do not silently treat the omission as a new stage-specific reward grant.
